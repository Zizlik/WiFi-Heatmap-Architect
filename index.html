Phones, laptops, and Wi-Fi speed

The same location may give different speeds. Devices differ in their antennas, supported bands, number of spatial streams, channel widths, and drivers.

A Wi-Fi 6 or Wi-Fi 7 label alone does not tell you what speed you will measure. That is why estimates use tests from your specific device. A phone test is not a speed guarantee for a laptop.

Internet speed is limited by the slowest part of the connection. Signal strength in dBm, Wi-Fi link speed, and actual download or upload speeds are different measurements.

A 50/50 target means reaching the target in separate tests of both directions. It does not guarantee simultaneous transfers or a minimum speed at all times.

Measure your own device

Use the same device and band. Record signal in dBm and run download and upload tests in several locations. Save them with Measure point.

Without tests the map only shows an approximate signal model. Speed estimates require at least two different signal strengths. Moving the router uses the original tests as a reference; it does not create new measurements.
